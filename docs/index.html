From confounding to trajectories

How a Causal Survival Forest (CSF) removes confounding, and how CAST extends it to a full treatment-effect trajectory — on simulated oncology data where the true effect is known.

Each patient cohort below is simulated, so we know the true average treatment effect (ATE) at every horizon (the solid black curve). The estimand is the RMST difference (extra restricted mean survival months from treatment) at horizons 12–120 months, in a synthetic cohort with control median survival near 55 months. Two illustrative effect shapes are available: a plateau (benefit that rises and levels off) and a reversal (a stylized early-benefit, late-harm crossing, not an empirical curve). Each cohort carries four confounders (age, stage, performance status, comorbidity count) that drive both prognosis and treatment assignment, a prognostic non-confounder (smoking, which worsens survival but does not affect who is treated), and two negative-control covariates (sex, ethnicity) that affect nothing. Accuracy vs. truth (RMSE, months)

Lower is better. Watch Naive collapse as confounding rises while CSF stays accurate. CSF and CAST are scored in-sample (no train/test split), so their accuracy here is optimistic relative to real-world deployment.

Cox baseline

A single time-constant hazard ratio, with no trajectory. The proportional-hazards test flags when a single hazard ratio cannot hold across all horizons.

Confounder imbalance (SMD)

Standardized mean difference between arms. |SMD|>0.1 signals imbalance the naive comparison cannot fix. As confounding rises, the four confounders become imbalanced while smoking (a non-confounder) and the negative controls sex and ethnicity stay near zero.

CAST trajectory

A smooth quadratic effect trajectory. The fit uses GLS with the inverse shrunk covariance when that is reliable, and falls back to a weighted fit when near-collinear horizons would otherwise destabilize it.

The shaded 95% band is conditional on the fitted forests: it comes from the bootstrap cross-horizon covariance with the forests held fixed, so it may understate total uncertainty because it does not propagate the forests' own estimation error. Peak time and effect are model-based summaries of the horizon-specific causal estimates, not separately identified causal quantities.

Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage

Shrinkage intensity λ and the cross-horizon covariance condition number, before → after shrinkage, that makes the GLS inverse stable.

1 · The confounding problem

Healthier patients (younger, earlier-stage, better performance status, fewer comorbidities) are preferentially selected into treatment (confounding by indication). The Naive unadjusted RMST difference then measures treatment effect plus baseline prognosis, so it is biased upward and overstates benefit. At zero confounding it matches the truth; turn confounding up and it diverges.

2 · CSF adjusts for confounding

The Causal Survival Forest orthogonalizes against a propensity model and a censoring model (Neyman-orthogonal, like double machine learning), recovering the true ATE at each horizon across confounding levels. The RSF S-learner — a plain survival forest with treatment as a feature — is shown as an ML baseline that is not orthogonalized and carries regularization bias.

3 · Baselines fall short

Cox gives one hazard ratio for all time and assumes proportional hazards; it cannot express a rising-then-plateauing or reversing effect. RSF predicts survival but gives no principled causal contrast.

4 · CAST extends CSF to a trajectory

CSF gives correlated point estimates across horizons. CAST bootstraps the cross-horizon covariance (patient-level), stabilizes it with Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage, and fits a GLS quadratic in time: β̂ = (XᵀΣ̂⁻¹X)⁻¹XᵀΣ̂⁻¹y. The result is a smooth effect trajectory whose clinical metrics (peak effect, time-to-peak) are model-based summaries of the per-horizon causal estimates rather than separately identified causal quantities.

Method provenance. The CSF fits use grf::causal_survival_forest (RMST target). The CAST layer (ledoit_wolf_shrinkage, bootstrap cross-horizon covariance, and the GLS quadratic fit) is ported directly from the production glioma CAST pipeline. All cohorts are simulated — no patient data.
